What is a thithi?

A day by the moon

A thithi is a "moon day". It is the time it takes the moon to move 12 degrees further away from the sun in the sky.1

There are 30 thithis from one new moon to the next.1 The first 15 are when the moon is growing (Shukla, or Valarpirai), ending at full moon (Pournami). The next 15 are when the moon is shrinking (Krishna, or Theipirai), ending at new moon (Amavasai).

A thithi does not line up with a normal day. It lasts about 20 to 27 hours,1 and can start or end at any time of day or night.

Why it matters for remembrance

Families remember a loved one on the thithi they passed away on, not on the same calendar date.2 So the date moves each year.

The yearly date is the same thithi in the same Tamil month as the passing.2,3 Tamil months follow the sun, and start when the sun moves into a new star sign.4

In the first year, many families also remember them every month, on the same thithi each month.5

How this site picks the day

  1. It finds the thithi at the time of passing. A passing before sunrise counts as the day before.2
  2. It finds that thithi in the right Tamil month.
  3. If the thithi covers two days, it picks the day it covers in the afternoon (aparahna), the time traditionally kept for these rites.2,3

Sunrise and sunset change with place. That is why the date where they lived and the date in their hometown can be one day apart.

If you don't know the time of passing, noon is used. The date may then be one day off, so please check it.

Please check with your priest

Families and regions follow different customs. This site uses one common method (Thirukanitha) for everyone. We have checked it against published dates,3,6 but your family priest has the final say.
